Persona Research & Accuracy Analysis

Are the PCP wireframe personas realistic? Real job data, survey statistics, and design implications.

3 Personas Assessed 30+ Sources BLS · CompTIA · Salesforce State of Sales · Glassdoor · LinkedIn Updated Feb 2026
← Partner Program Research
Research Methodology: Personas were assessed against publicly available job posting data, BLS occupational statistics, CompTIA's 2024 State of the Channel (n=1,042 channel company executives), Salesforce State of Sales 7th Edition (n=7,400+ sales professionals), Glassdoor compensation data, and industry analyst publications. Where data was behind a paywall, only publicly accessible summaries and cited statistics were used.
⚠ Proto-personas — not yet research-validated. These personas are grounded in secondary research (job postings, published surveys, BLS data) but have not been validated through primary user research — no interviews, recruitment screeners, or moderated studies have been conducted. Treat them as informed hypotheses, not validated portraits. They should be revisited before making feature prioritization or design decisions that depend heavily on user goals or pain points.

What's Accurate

  • Job title "Account Executive" is the most common title at mid-market SIs and VARs
  • CloudBridge Solutions as a mid-market systems integrator is a completely realistic company type
  • Quota + commission structure is standard; 50/50 or 60/40 base/variable is industry norm
  • Pain points — portal friction, admin overload, slow approvals — are strongly backed by survey data
  • 84% of firms satisfied with vendor programs overall (CompTIA 2024), but portal UX is still the #1 barrier to rep adoption

Recommended Enrichments

  • Show explicit quota attainment widget — this is what actually motivates login
  • Surface commission tracking on deals (pending approval, approved, paid) in the deal registration view
  • Reference the 28% selling stat as rationale for minimizing portal admin friction
  • Add a "Quick Register" flow from mobile — reps are in front of customers
  • Emphasize deal status transparency: "Will I get credit for this?" is the #1 concern

What's Accurate

  • The role itself is real — someone at every partner company manages portal access and deal registration
  • Responsibilities listed in the wireframe (invite reps, assign training, track deals) match documented role requirements
  • Pain points — being caught between reps and vendor, data quality issues — are consistent with Operations role research
  • Working at a mid-market SI is realistic; this is where the admin burden is highest and dedicated portal staff least likely

What Needs Revision

  • "Portal Administrator" as a standalone job title is too narrow — this is almost never a dedicated role at a partner company of CloudBridge's size
  • More realistic title: "Sales Operations Manager" or "Partner Operations Manager"
  • The role is likely a 40–60% portion of someone's broader Sales Ops or RevOps responsibilities
  • Seniority may be slightly underrepresented — this person often holds a Manager-level title and may report to a VP of Sales
  • Add the frustration of having no good reporting tools for measuring their own program's health

Design Implications

  • Admin dashboard should lead with adoption metrics (who's logged in, deal reg volume, training %) — this is what Jordan has to report upward
  • Bulk operations are critical: invite 20 reps at once, bulk assign training, export reports to Excel for leadership decks
  • Surface data quality indicators: highlight deals with incomplete fields or duplicates so Jordan can fix them proactively
  • Give Jordan an "executive summary" report she can export and share with her VP without reformatting

Bought into Equinix through CloudBridge and uses the EUC portal to manage active assets, track support tickets, and report SLA performance to internal stakeholders and finance.

Draft
Not yet researched
⚠ This is a proto-persona — draft only.

Maria's goals and pain points are inferred from industry knowledge about IT Directors at financial services firms that use Equinix colocation. No primary research has been conducted. Everything below should be treated as a hypothesis to test — not a validated finding. Do not use to justify design decisions without user research to back it up.

What We Can Reasonably Assume (Secondary Research)

  • Title range: IT Director, Director of Network Infrastructure, VP of Technology Operations, Head of Infrastructure — all common at mid-large financial firms
  • Equinix buying pattern: Financial services firms are a top Equinix vertical; colocation + Fabric is a common combo for regulated data residency + cloud connectivity needs
  • Procurement path: Often buys through a partner/SI for pricing, bundling, and support — not directly from Equinix sales
  • Internal reporting burden: IT Directors in regulated industries routinely report infrastructure spend to Finance and C-suite; cost allocation and chargeback are real workflows
  • SLA accountability: Financial services SLAs are contractual; any outage or deviation creates internal audit exposure

Hypothetical Pain Points (Unvalidated)

  • Provisioning status is opaque — relies on emailing CloudBridge rep for updates, no self-service tracking
  • SLA reports arrive as PDFs in quarterly emails; no real-time view or threshold alerting
  • Monthly Equinix invoices don't map to internal cost centers — Finance requests manual allocation every month
  • Support tickets feel like a black box after submission — no status visibility without calling support
  • Renewing or ordering new services requires a sales cycle through the partner rep, even for straightforward expansions

These pain points are plausible but not validated. Each one is a candidate research question for a first-round EUC interview script.

What Needs Primary Research

  • Does Maria log into the EUC portal at all, or does she delegate this entirely to a NOC or IT ops team member?
  • What does her self-service tolerance look like — does she want a portal, or does she prefer account-managed service?
  • How does she track SLA compliance today — portal, email, spreadsheet, third-party monitoring?
  • Who in her organization actually raises support tickets? Is Maria even the right target for the ticket-tracking workflow?
  • How important is cost allocation granularity — is this a pain point for her, or does Finance handle it separately?

Design Implications (Tentative)

  • EUC portal homepage: asset overview with real-time status, open tickets, and a prominent SLA compliance indicator
  • Provisioning tracker: step-by-step status visible without contacting the rep or Equinix support
  • Billing: cost-by-site or cost-by-service breakdown, exportable for Finance chargeback
  • Support: ticket history and real-time status — not just confirmation emails
  • The right level of self-service vs. rep-assisted is unknown — don't over-invest in self-service order flows until Maria's workflow is validated

🔍 Design Implications from Research For the PCP

For Steve (Rep Portal)

Deal registration is not administrative — it's commission. Every second of friction before a deal is locked costs Steve money. Mobile-first deal reg, instant confirmation, and real-time status tracking are the features that will actually drive portal login. Collateral search during customer calls is the secondary use case.

For Jordan (PM Admin Portal)

Jordan needs to prove the program works upward to her VP. Her primary portal use is reporting and bulk operations, not day-to-day selling. The admin dashboard should lead with exportable adoption metrics — and every workflow should have a bulk path (bulk invite, bulk assign training, bulk export).

For Marcus (PSM Portal — Equinix-side)

Marcus manages 5–15 partners and needs to see across all of them without drilling into each record. A partner portfolio view — showing health score, pipeline, training completion, and last QBR date — is the primary workflow. Deal dispute resolution needs a structured workflow, not an email chain.

For Maria (EUC Portal) — Tentative

Maria's primary need is visibility, not self-service purchasing. A homepage showing asset health, open ticket count, and current SLA compliance — without drilling into sub-pages — is likely the highest-value design target. Cost allocation export is a secondary but recurring workflow. Validate before building; Maria may delegate portal use entirely to NOC or IT ops staff.

Why Partners Abandon Portals

CompTIA data: 50% of partners dropped a vendor for poor experience in the last 2 years. xAmplify research: 47% cite usability and integration as the top barrier. Fragmented tools (portal + CRM + email + LMS) reduce adoption 40–55%. The single biggest win is reducing tool-switching — ideally, one place for deals, training, and collateral.

Portal Adoption Is Driven by Self-Interest

Steve logs in when he can see his commission status. Jordan logs in when she can see her team's metrics. Marcus logs in when he can see pipeline across his book of business. Adoption is not about making the portal beautiful — it's about making the portal answer the question each persona has in their head when they open it.

Jordan's Title Should Be Revised

Calling Jordan a "Portal Administrator" implies a dedicated, narrow role that rarely exists in practice. Renaming to "Sales Operations Manager" or "Partner Operations Manager" better reflects how this responsibility is actually distributed at mid-market SIs — and changes the assumptions designers make about her seniority, access level, and goals.
