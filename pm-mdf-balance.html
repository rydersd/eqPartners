Design Notes — pm-mdf-balance.html

Purpose

The MDF Balance screen is Steve Rogers's first stop before planning any co-marketing activity. It answers the question he'd otherwise have to email Alex (his PAM) to ask: "How much MDF do I actually have left this quarter?"

Without this screen, the flow is: Steve logs in → submits a request → PAM rejects it because the balance is lower than Steve thought → Steve is frustrated. With this screen, he self-serves the budget check, plans accordingly, and submits a clean request.

Jobs to Be Done

  • AE-2: Pursue joint marketing opportunities — Steve must understand available budget before planning anything.
  • Secondary: give Steve a paper trail of approved vs. committed vs. spent so he can explain MDF status to his own leadership without asking Equinix.

Key Design Decisions

Balance vs. Available vs. Committed

The three-way split (Spent/Committed/Available) matters. Partners frequently assume "committed" funds are already gone, or confuse "approved but not yet executed" requests with paid claims. The labeled bar makes all three states visually distinct at a glance.

Expiry countdown

Quarter-end expiry is the #1 driver of rushed, low-quality MDF submissions. Surfacing the countdown prominently with the submit-by deadline (Mar 17 to allow approval time before Mar 31 expiry) nudges Steve to plan earlier. This directly reduces last-minute request volume for Alex's approval queue.

Rejected request visible in table

Showing MDF-2026-002 (rejected — internal-only) is intentional. Rejected requests are a learning tool. Steve can see why it was rejected without having to dig through email, which reduces re-submissions of ineligible activities.

Historical utilization

Q3 FY2025's 61% dip is represented as a visual warning. In the real system this is where Marcus (PSM) or Alex (PAM) would have a note attached ("Program check-in triggered"). For wireframe purposes it signals that low utilization has consequences, which motivates consistent MDF use.

Flow Position

This screen sits before pm-mdf-request.html in the user journey. The Quick Actions sidebar links forward to Request, POP, and Co-Marketing.

Full MDF flow: pm-mdf-balance → pm-mdf-request → pm-mdf-comarketing → pam-mdf-queue → activity → pm-pop-submission → psm-pop-review → pam-mdf-claims
