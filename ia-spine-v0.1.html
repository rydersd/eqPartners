Purpose & Principles

  • This Spine defines the stable backbone of the experience — not a full sitemap or spec. Use it to align stakeholders, onboard designers, and arbitrate scope disputes.
  • PAM · Alex owns financial gates and tier enforcement. PSM · Marcus owns partner health and co-sell relationships. Never merge their queues into a single review surface.
  • Experience Cloud is the partner-facing surface. Lightning is the internal surface. Data flows one direction: Experience Cloud → Lightning. Approvals flow back.
  • Before adding a screen, check Section 4 (Handoff Seams). Most "missing features" are seam problems, not nav problems.
  • [A] = Assumption not yet validated. [?] = Open question requiring a decision. Do not implement either without resolution.

Governance & Evolution Rules

  • Version format: vX.Y — Increment X for structural changes (new seam, new canonical object). Increment Y for clarifications or assumption resolution.
  • Change trigger: Any new screen proposal must identify which JTBD it serves and which seam it belongs to. If neither, escalate to IA lead before design begins.
  • Decision log: Record each structural decision as [DATE] [DECISION] [OWNER] [RATIONALE]. Append below — do not edit prior entries.
  • Assumption resolution: When an [A] is validated or invalidated, update this document and increment the minor version. Same when a [?] is answered.
  • Retirement rule: A nav item or canonical object is removed only when zero JTBDs reference it. Confirm in writing before removing from the Spine.

Design Notes
What this page is

The IA Spine is the design-team source of truth for the Lead-to-Cash experience. It is not a sitemap or a spec — it is the backbone that keeps both documents coherent.

Audience: IA Lead, UX Designers, Product Managers, CRM Architects. Not intended for partners or AEs.

JTBD references

JTBDs in Section 2 use IDs (PAM-1, PSM-4, etc.) that anchor to workflows-jtbd.html. These IDs must remain stable — do not renumber without updating all downstream links.

Canonical objects — key rule

Every object in Section 3 has exactly one System of Record. If a team proposes storing the same data in two places, this table is the first place to look to resolve the dispute.
