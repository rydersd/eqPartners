⚙ Wireframe Spec — dealdesk-queue.html

Persona

Rachel Kim — Deal Desk Analyst, Equinix. Internal Operations

Rachel processes special pricing exception requests that PAMs submit when a deal requires discounts above the standard cap, non-standard contract terms, compensation overrides, or deal timeline extensions. She sits between the PAM layer and Finance/VP approval, and her decisions directly affect whether deals can be priced to close against competition.

Why Deal Desk was a blind spot

The existing wireframe set showed PAMs requesting deal exceptions (via a stub link) and partners seeing their deal status. But no screen existed for the person who actually reviews and approves exceptions. This created a logical gap: where do exception requests go? Who sees them? What information do they need to decide?

Design decisions

  • SLA countdown on urgent items: Deal Desk operates under strict time-bound SLAs — a PAM submitting a 4-hour urgent request needs a decision before the customer closes. Visible countdowns on urgent rows create genuine urgency without requiring Rachel to open every record to find what's on fire.
  • Inline detail drawer (no page navigation): Deal Desk analysts review 15-25 requests per day. Opening a new page per request breaks flow. The inline drawer exposes the PAM's justification, key figures, and a decision form without leaving the queue — analogous to Gmail's conversation expand pattern.
  • Approve/More Info/Deny/Escalate as a set: "More Info" is a real fourth state — Rachel often needs competitive pricing data or historical discount context before she can approve or deny. Surfacing this as a first-class button prevents the workaround of emailing PAMs outside the system.
  • Over-cap flag at a glance: Discount vs. cap shown in the table (e.g., "38% req. (cap: 32%)") lets Rachel instantly triage whether a request requires an override or is within standard bounds. Within-cap requests can be approved in a single click without opening the drawer.
  • Approval rate KPI: A 62% approval rate visible to the analyst contextualizes the queue. If the rate trends too high (rubber-stamp behavior) or too low (blocking deals), it surfaces in the KPI strip for management review.
  • Regional scope in nav: The header shows "Americas Region" — Deal Desk is typically regionalized. Rachel should not see EMEA/APJ requests unless she's cross-regional.
  • Gray/charcoal role badge and ctx-badge: Deal Desk is internal ops — neutral gray distinguishes it from Equinix-facing roles (PSM blue, PAM rust, CMM amber) and partner-facing roles (red, teal, purple). It signals "behind the scenes."

Exception types handled

  • Special Pricing: Non-standard product/bundle pricing. Requires VP Finance sign-off over a threshold.
  • Over-Cap Discount: Requested discount exceeds tier cap (e.g., Gold tier cap is 32%; partner needs 38%).
  • Non-Standard Terms: Custom SLA, payment terms, early exit clauses, or non-standard data residency requirements.
  • Comp Override: Adjusts how partner rep compensation is calculated — common when a deal crosses fiscal quarters or involves a restructured territory.
  • Deal Extension: Pushes the deal's close date in Salesforce — required when a procurement delay is not the partner's fault.

Stub screens introduced

  • dealdesk-approved.html — approved request archive
  • dealdesk-denied.html — denied request archive with rationale history
  • dealdesk-analytics.html — approval rates, avg decision time, exception type breakdown
  • dealdesk-guidelines.html — current discount caps, term policies, escalation thresholds

Upstream and downstream connections

  • Upstream: pam-deal-exceptions.html (stub) — PAM submits the exception request here
  • Upstream: psm-deal-register.html — deal context visible in the drawer
  • Downstream: pam-home-dashboard.html — PAM sees decision notification in their activity feed
  • Downstream: pm-deal-registration.html — partner sees updated deal terms if approved

Accessibility notes

  • Queue table uses scope="col" on all headers for screen reader column association.
  • Tabs use proper role="tablist", role="tab", aria-selected, and aria-controls.
  • Inline drawer expand/collapse buttons carry aria-expanded state updates via JS.
  • Countdown timers are text-based (not CSS-only) for screen reader compatibility.
  • Priority is conveyed by both color chip and text label — not color alone.
