Design Notes — pm-exec-dashboard.html

Purpose

Diane Reeves is VP Sales at CloudBridge. She doesn't register deals or submit MDF requests — Steve and Jordan handle that. But she needs to know: what's our Equinix tier status, how much pipeline are we running through them, what's our MDF burn rate, and are we on track for Gold renewal?

Without this screen, Diane either asks Jordan for a status email (Jordan's time) or logs into the full portal and tries to piece it together from multiple screens (Diane's time). Neither is a good use of anyone's time before a QBR or board update.

New Persona: Diane Reeves (VP Sales, CloudBridge)

Not represented in the original four personas. Diane is:

  • A read-only stakeholder — she shouldn't be able to submit requests or modify data
  • Quarterly-cadence — she looks at this before QBRs, board meetings, and partner renewal reviews
  • Focused on revenue, tier status, and relationship health — not operational details
  • Likely has a counterpart at every partner org. This is a commonly missed persona in PRM design.

Design Decisions

Read-only, intentionally scoped

The callout at the bottom explicitly tells Diane this is a summary view and that operational actions live elsewhere. This prevents confusion about her access level and sets expectations correctly.

Tier attainment as a visual bar

Diane's #1 question before a QBR is "are we on track for Gold?" The tier requirements panel shows exactly where they stand vs. each requirement. Sourced ARR at 70% and MDF utilization at 68% (below 80% requirement) are the two things she needs to action before June 30.

Linked out to operational detail

Every section has a "details →" link into the operational screen for whoever needs to dig deeper. Exec sees summary; AE/Admin sees full detail.

Access Model

Diane has a separate Experience Cloud profile with an "Exec" permission set that gives her read access to aggregate data but no write permissions. Jordan (Admin) provisions this role via pm-role-permissions.html.
