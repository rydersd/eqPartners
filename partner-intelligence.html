Partner Intelligence · Signal Layer

The portal knows more
than it shows.

Equinix has a partner data graph that no single person — and no current screen — can see in full. Deal velocity. MDF dispute patterns. API health. Training completion. Tier trajectory. Program eligibility. The signal layer makes that graph visible, contextually, exactly where each persona is working. Not a dashboard. Not a chatbot. Just the two or three things worth knowing right now.

The problem: four silos, zero synthesis

Every persona sees their slice. Nobody sees the whole partner.

Steve sees his pipeline. Alex sees her approval queue. Marcus sees his 15 partner accounts. Jordan sees the org's training completion rate. Each of them has exactly enough context to do their own job — and not enough to notice what's about to go wrong in someone else's.

A Fabric Attach signal lives in Salesforce CRM data. A churn-risk signal lives in deal velocity and contact change data. A dispute-pattern signal lives in the MDF ledger. None of those signals currently cross the boundary into the screens where they would change behavior.

Partner Intelligence Graph — what the signal layer requires
A unified view of each partner's commercial relationship, technical health, program eligibility, and learning trajectory — computed, not queried. This is the data architecture prerequisite. The signal layer is the design hypothesis for what becomes possible when it exists.

Four signal types. One visual language.

Signals are typed so users develop instincts about what each color means.

⚡
Program Signal
Active Equinix program is relevant here
Surfaced when an active Equinix partner program — Fabric Attach, Network Edge Push, Marketplace Featured, etc. — is directly applicable to what's on screen. The signal translates program strategy into deal-level or account-level action.
Example (deal registration): "84% of comparable deals include Fabric. Partners who attach close 2.3× faster. This deal doesn't have Fabric on it."
⚠
Risk Detected
Something may go wrong or already is
Surfaced when pattern matching detects friction, churn risk, compliance gaps, or SLA exposure. Shown to the persona best positioned to act — not broadcast to everyone.
Example (MDF approval queue): "CloudBridge has disputed 3 of their last 5 claims. All three involved event sponsorship. Their next claim is in this queue."
↑
Opportunity
A specific upside action is available now
Surfaced when timing and context align for a positive action that might otherwise be missed. Time-sensitive. Actionable. Always specific — not generic encouragement.
Example (PAM dashboard): "DataBridge Corp qualified for Gold tier 18 days ago but hasn't been notified. Tier upgrade unlocks 5% incremental margin for them."
◎
Insight
A pattern worth knowing, neutrally stated
Surfaced when the system has noticed something that doesn't require immediate action but changes how the persona should read the current situation. No urgency. No judgment. Just: here's what we noticed.
Example (PSM QBR prep): "CloudBridge's deal velocity dropped 40% after January 12th. Their primary contact, Sarah Chen, left the company on that date."

The Fabric Attach scenario — a program becomes a signal

How one Equinix strategic program translates into contextual intelligence across the partner journey.

Scenario · Fabric Attach Program

Equinix wants Fabric on every deal. The partner portal doesn't know that.

Imagine Equinix runs a strategic program: attach Equinix Fabric to every eligible deal. Internally, this exists as a Sales Play, a training module, a quota component. But in the partner portal, where the deals are actually registered, there is no signal. Steve registers a $200K Colocation deal. No one and nothing tells him Fabric belongs on it. The signal layer fixes this.

1
pm-deal-registration.html
Steve is registering a $200K Colocation deal for Meridian Financial. He selects "Colocation" as product type and moves to the next step.
⚡ Program Signal appears
"84% of Colocation deals over $150K include Fabric. Partners who attach Fabric retain at 1.8× the rate and close 2.3× faster. Meridian Financial has 3 existing Colo circuits — all Fabric-eligible. Add Fabric to this deal?"
2
pam-home-dashboard.html · Alex Rivera's view
Steve adds Fabric. The deal comes to Alex for approval. Alex's PAM dashboard shows a signal for her portfolio, not just this deal.
◎ Insight appears
"CloudBridge's Fabric attach rate is 31% — below the 58% Gold tier average. 4 open deals in their pipeline are Fabric-eligible but unattached."
3
psm-qbr-prep.html · Marcus Chen's view
Marcus is preparing for CloudBridge's quarterly review. A signal surfaces in his QBR prep screen connecting Fabric attach trajectory to tier risk.
⚠ Risk appears
"CloudBridge is 12% below the Gold tier Fabric attach threshold. At current trajectory they will miss the Q2 tier review requirement. QBR is in 8 days — this is the right moment to set a joint attach target."
4
pm-team-training.html · Jordan Reeves's view
Jordan is managing training assignments for the CloudBridge team. A signal surfaces that Fabric training completion correlates directly with attach rate.
↑ Opportunity appears
"3 AEs on your team haven't completed Fabric Certified Sales. Partners who complete this module increase attach rates by 2.1× within 60 days. Assign now?"

One program. Four screens. Four personas. Four different signals — each one specific to what that person can actually do about it. The signal layer is the mechanism by which Equinix's strategic intent becomes partner-facing intelligence without requiring anyone to navigate to a training module, read a program brief, or remember what the Q2 focus is.

The bridge to the lead-to-cash datastory

Signals are the spine of the narrative.

The lead-to-cash datastory project — an interactive, pseudo-realistic walkthrough of the Equinix partner journey from first contact to closed revenue — needs a backbone. The signal layer is that backbone. Each chapter of the datastory is anchored by a signal: what the system noticed, what changed, what someone did about it.

Chapter 1: Steve registers a Colocation deal. A Fabric Attach signal appears. He adds Fabric. Deal velocity: +2.3×. Chapter 2: Alex sees a dispute-pattern risk signal on CloudBridge. She adds documentation guidance to their next MDF submission template. Disputes drop. Chapter 3: Marcus walks into the QBR with a pre-computed attach-trajectory signal. He sets a joint target with the partner. Tier retained.

The signals don't require sensitive data. They require constructed, plausible, pseudo-realistic data that makes the story legible — numbers that feel true even if they aren't pulled from a live system. That's the datastory model: build the experience first, connect the data pipes later.
